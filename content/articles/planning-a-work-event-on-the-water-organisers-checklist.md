Somebody has handed you the team day, the client evening or the awards night,
and somebody else has said "what about a boat". A boat is a good answer. It is
also a venue with a departure time, which changes how you plan it. This is the
list to work through, in the order the decisions actually come.

If you already know you want the whole vessel, the page that takes the enquiry
is [corporate yacht charter on the Gold Coast](/corporate-yacht-charter). The
rest of this is for getting your own thinking straight first.

## 1. Decide what the event is for

It sounds obvious and it decides everything after it. A boat does three kinds of
work event well, and they want different things:

- **A thank you.** Drinks, food, a view, no agenda. The simplest to run and the
  hardest to get wrong.
- **Client entertainment.** The same, with more attention on arrival, hosting
  and how people get home.
- **An event with a programme.** Awards, a launch, a presentation, a team
  session. This needs a room that works for speaking, so it needs the
  conversation about screens and sound early.

Write the answer down in one line before you enquire. It is the first thing you
will be asked.

## 2. Get the headcount to within ten

You do not need a final number. You need the right bracket, because the bracket
picks the vessel:

- **Sun Goddess** is 114 feet and carries up to 135 guests on a day charter,
  across two entertainment decks with two licensed bars.
- **Mermaid Spirit** is a 100 foot tri-deck catamaran and carries up to 150 by
  day, with a chef's kitchen on board.

A whole-vessel charter suits groups from about 20 up to 150.

**If you have 20 to 60 people and it is drinks rather than a private event**,
you may not need a charter at all. The Luxury Scenic Sunset Cruise runs on
Saturdays and Sundays with 100 seats a sailing, at $79 an adult, and can be
booked seat by seat. You share the vessel. For relaxed team drinks that is
often fine; for a client evening or anything with speeches it is not.

## 3. Fix the time before you fix anything else

A restaurant lets latecomers drift in. A boat leaves.

Put the boarding time in the invitation, not the departure time, and say plainly
that the vessel departs on schedule. Then work backwards: if people are coming
from an office, how long does it really take them to get to Main Beach at that
hour, and is anyone presenting who cannot be late?

The same applies at the other end. Everyone finishes at the same moment in the
same place, which is a gift for the organiser as long as the way home has been
thought about.

## 4. Sort out how people get there and back

Both vessels depart Marine Stadium Jetty & Pontoon at Doug Jennings Park, Main
Beach, about ten minutes from Surfers Paradise and fifteen from Broadbeach.
There is free parking at the pontoon.

For a group, maxi taxis are the usual choice and the most cost-effective one.
If drinks are being served, arrange the return transport before the day rather
than at the pontoon afterwards, and tell people what it is.

## 5. If anyone is speaking, say so when you enquire

Both vessels have flat-screen televisions and audio throughout. That covers a
welcome, a toast, an awards run and a short presentation.

What it needs from you is notice. Say at the time of enquiry what you intend to
show and who needs a microphone, so the answer you get is about your event
rather than a general yes. Bring your slides on something that does not depend
on a mobile signal.

Company branding, custom menus and event theming can be arranged. Ask early,
because signage has a lead time that the boat does not control.

## 6. Choose the food by what people will be doing

Catering is built around the event with the vessel's culinary partners, from
grazing boards and canapes through to private chef dining, and bar packages are
customisable. External food and drink is not permitted on board, so this is
decided with the operator rather than around them.

A useful rule: if people are meant to mix, feed them standing. If people are
meant to listen, feed them seated, before or after the speaking and not during
it. Collect dietary requirements with the RSVP, not the week before.

## 7. Ask the accessibility question before you book

If anyone in the group has limited mobility, raise it before you confirm so the
specific requirements can be assessed against the vessel and the pontoon. It is
a short conversation when it happens first and an awkward one when it happens on
the day.

## 8. Get the wet weather plan in writing

Ask what happens if the forecast turns, and ask for the answer in writing with
the booking. You are the person who will be asked on the morning.

## What to send when you enquire

Five things make the first reply useful:

1. What the event is for, in one line.
2. A date, or two or three candidates.
3. A headcount bracket.
4. Roughly how long you want to be out.
5. Whether anyone is presenting, and the kind of food you have in mind.

Charters are quoted rather than listed, because the figure moves with every one
of those. With all five, the quote is a short conversation.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many people do you need to book a boat for a work event?

A whole-vessel charter suits groups from about 20 up to 150. Sun Goddess carries
up to 135 guests on a day charter and Mermaid Spirit up to 150. Smaller groups
who do not need the vessel to themselves can book seats on the Luxury Scenic
Sunset Cruise instead.

### Can you give a presentation on a boat?

Yes. Both vessels have flat-screen televisions and audio throughout. Say what
you intend to show and who needs a microphone when you enquire, so the setup is
confirmed for your event.

### Can we bring our own food and drinks?

No. External food and beverages are not permitted on board. Catering and bar
packages are arranged through the operator and built around the event.

### Where do guests board, and is there parking?

At Marine Stadium Jetty & Pontoon, Doug Jennings Park, Main Beach, about ten
minutes from Surfers Paradise. There is free parking at the pontoon, and maxi
taxis are the usual choice for groups.

### What should the invitation say?

The boarding time rather than the departure time, the boarding location, how
people are getting home, and a line saying the vessel departs on schedule.
